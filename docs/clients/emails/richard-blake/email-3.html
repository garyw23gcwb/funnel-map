One behaviour comes up in the accounts of estranged parents so often that I gave it a name in the book.

The searching.

Checking your child's social media. Looking at their photographs. Reading what they've posted.

Often after midnight. Often while telling yourself you'll stop.

Here's what's going on.

The brain's attachment system keeps you moving towards someone who was once a primary source of connection. It keeps doing it after they've gone.

The searching is what a nervous system that loved someone and lost them does when it has not yet received a signal that it is safe to stop looking.

Recognising this is not a criticism. It is an invitation to be gentle with yourself.

There is a cost, though.

Each check gives you just enough closeness to stop you adjusting. The loss can't fully register while the search goes on.

So what do you do when the urge comes? To check, to text, to explain it one more time, properly?

I call that urge love with nowhere to go. And I have one simple move for it.

Name it. Then wait a night.

Naming it turns the volume down. Sleep decides the rest.
